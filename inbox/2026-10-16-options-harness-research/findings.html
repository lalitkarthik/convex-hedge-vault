An agent harness for options research

Findings page · 16 October 2026 · merged from five research lanes and López de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning (AFML). Raw research capture in the vault inbox: staging, not fact. Everything marked "proposal" is for Lalit and Bilal to decide.

The short answer. There is no complete, real-quality options research harness in public. The serious options benchmark (LiveOption, September 2026) finds that an LLM that trades options freely has no edge. So we cannot copy a system. We must assemble one from the general quant harnesses, the coding harnesses, and AFML, and add the options parts ourselves.

Every harness that works follows three rules. Our design follows them too.

1. The fenceThe agent edits one small surface: a strategy spec or a feature. Data, labels, splits, the cost model and the scorer are frozen code that no agent can change. When this fence was missing, one agent edited its own time limit, and another chose a sample that skipped a drawdown.
2. A separate judgeThe role that judges must not be the role that built. Builders praise their own work. The judge reads code, traces and the ledger, but not the author's reasoning. It re-runs the work and can veto it. It is tested on planted bugs before we trust it.
3. Memory on diskEvery trial goes into an append-only ledger, failures included. Each batch of agents starts with a clean context and reads the ledger. The ledger gives the true trial count that the deflated Sharpe needs, and it is the audit trail.
The finding to remember. A 2026 paper (Gençay, arXiv 2608.27734) planted a look-ahead "oracle" in an agent loop. It posted a Sharpe ratio of 34.7 and a deflated Sharpe of 1.00, so it passed the statistics. In the same paper, the best honest LLM-found strategy after 102 trials had an in-sample Sharpe of 1.69, a deflated Sharpe of 0.86 and an out-of-sample Sharpe of 0.18. Deflation catches selection among honest backtests. It cannot see leaked data. That is why the fence (rule 1) comes first and the statistics come second. I checked both numbers at the source.

1. The workflow: roles and handoffs (proposal)

This merges the agent-harness lane's proposal with AFML's "research factory" stations (AFML ch. 1). AFML has no idea scout and no separate skeptic: it assumes that humans bring ideas, and its checks live inside the backtest statistics. The agent sources show that a harness needs both. 2. The validation gate (proposal)

A strategy reaches a human only when it passes every step in order. The first failure stops it, and every result goes to the ledger. Two adaptations run through all of it. The unit of independence is the trading day, not the 30-minute window. Performance is measured on daily P&L, with its real skew and fat tails.

  1. Preregistration. The hypothesis card, with its trial budget, is hashed before the agent touches evaluation data.
  2. No look-ahead by construction. Features come only from the registry. A truncation test passes: cut the data at time t and recompute, and every earlier value must stay the same. A planted-oracle canary must be flagged.
  3. Data hygiene. Quote age, crossed and locked quotes, strikes as listed at decision time, the right settlement rule for each market and date.
  4. Costs. The edge survives the spread at a realistic fill, and it survives 2× costs.
  5. Deflation. Raw and clustered trial counts. Deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95 on daily P&L. For a broad search, also t ≥ 3 (Harvey–Liu–Zhu).
  6. PBO ≤ 0.2 through CSCV on the family's daily P&L.
  7. CPCV with whole-day groups, purging and at least one day of embargo (for NIFTY, the label length). At least 80% of paths are positive.
  8. Tails and track record. CVaR 95 and 99, the worst day ÷ premium, maximum drawdown, the Omega curve, the 5 worst days replayed. The minimum track-record length fits inside the sample.
  9. Strategy risk (AFML ch. 15). The probability that the hit rate falls below the break-even rate is ≤ 0.05, with the spread paid.
  10. Robustness. The sign holds in each volatility tercile and in both half-samples. Neighbouring parameters also work: a plateau, not a peak.
  11. Frozen holdout, scored once, on days nobody has downloaded yet. The result comes back only as pass or fail.
  12. Calibrated skeptic in a fresh context. It re-runs the work and can veto.

The 0.2 and 80% thresholds are the lane's starting points, to tune. No paper applies PBO, CPCV or deflated Sharpe to intraday or 0DTE options, so these adaptations are reasoning from AFML and from our 8 October method, not tested practice.

Why Sharpe misleads for short volatility

Short-volatility books win small and often, and lose large rarely. Lo's "Capital Decimation Partners" sold out-of-the-money puts from 1992 to 1999 and showed a Sharpe ratio of 1.94. Goetzmann et al. (2007) prove that option trades can raise the Sharpe ratio with no skill at all. The probabilistic Sharpe ratio corrects for skew and kurtosis:

PSR = Z[ (SR − SR*)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR²) ]

Negative skew (γ₃ < 0) and fat tails (γ₄ > 3) make the denominator larger, so PSR falls. Here is an illustration with assumed numbers. Take a daily Sharpe of 0.1 (about 1.6 a year) with skew −3 and kurtosis 20. At 95% confidence it needs about 366 trading days, which is more than one year of SPXW data. One year cannot prove a short-volatility claim of that size.

3. What the backtester must model (options, not stocks)

A stock engine holds one price series. An options engine holds a chain: at each step, a grid of strike × expiry × call/put whose contracts are listed and expire. The backtester lane compared about a dozen engines. These are the parts we must get right, ordered by how often existing engines get them wrong.

4. The seed idea queue: options alpha axes

An axis is one independent reason an options position makes money. The alpha lane wrote 19 axes as typed cards, each with a hypothesis, who loses, the features, the data, a test in our bucket method, and a kill line. These cards are the idea scout's starting backlog. An idea the scout finds later is an instance of an axis, such as a clock time or an event type.

One fact frames all of it. Inside the SPXW 0DTE day, the pooled ratio of realised to charged variance is 1.01–1.02 on our year of data. So "options are dear" adds nothing inside the day. An axis must say when or which options are dear.

The evidence calls these myths: max pain at index level; the public put-call ratio as a timing signal; vanna and charm "driving the market" (no academic test found); dispersion and parity violations as edges for us; and Greek ratios as new information. Run max pain and the put-call ratio once each as negative controls. If the harness finds an edge there, suspect the harness.

What your ratio Δ²/(2Γθ) measures

With zero rates, the delta-hedged Black–Scholes equation gives θ = −½σ²S²Γ. For a straddle struck at K, the delta is about Γ·(S − K). Put these together:

Δ² / (2Γ|θ|) ≈ Γ²(S−K)² / (Γ²σ²S²) = (S−K)² / (σ²S²)

Divide by the elapsed time h, and you get the squared move since the strike over the implied variance for that time. That is the window's end-point move against what the market charged. The hedged seller's P&L instead sums the squared increments along the path. So the ratio, divided by (RV/IV)², is the Lo–MacKinlay variance ratio. It is above 1 when the window trended and below 1 when it chopped. This explains why it fits the unhedged seller well. It is a real feature, but it is "trendiness", not new Greek information. Test it once, under that name. I checked this algebra myself.

6. Jev: typed decisions inside the loop

Jev is a "System One" decision model from TypeSafe AI, released in September 2026. It does not write text. You send it a state and typed questions, and it returns typed answers with probabilities. There are three question types: Choice (pick one from a closed set), Score (place the state on a rubric you define) and Noul (the probability that a yes/no statement is true). A call costs about $0.00004 and takes about 0.3 s. Jev is a component that goes inside a harness; it is not a harness.

The rule every source repeats: "an LLM proposes, Jev answers, code decides, every step leaves a receipt." Hard rules and budgets run in code before any Jev call. A Jev answer can relax friction only toward a floor that code sets, and answers near 0.5 escalate to a reasoning model or a human. This is our fence and our ledger, applied to every small decision.

Jev is strong at local judgments about text. In one independent benchmark of 22,500 calls, reranking rose from MRR 0.62 to 0.84 and routing among 199 tools reached 96.5%. On prompt injection it is good but not enough alone: Red Hat measured 78% recall (checked), and injections hidden inside tool output were caught 85% of the time. It is weak at judging across steps: 51% on model-difficulty routing, and AUROC 0.56 (near random) on finding which step of a trajectory caused a failure. The one end-to-end A/B test is mixed. It filtered a coding agent's tool output through Jev: an early 30-task run favoured Jev (25 vs 22 passed), and a later run went the other way (2 vs 5).

The request format is now open. Ollama (since 0.35) and vLLM serve the same /v1/systemone request on local models, and TypeSafe's own adapter sends it to Claude. So one decide() function can send public text to hosted Jev and our internal text to a local model, with no change in code (checked).

7. A build order, if you build it

Kinlay's order and the MCP lane agree: trust comes before speed.

  1. The trial ledger and the single evaluation entry point. Without an honest trial count, the deflated Sharpe and PBO mean nothing.
  2. The SPXW data contract and chain-data: settlement, the expiry-time fix, our own IV, quote age.
  3. The frozen backtester: costs by leg count, whole-day purged splits, CPCV, diagnostics. Then the truncation test and the oracle canary.
  4. The critic, calibrated on planted defects before anything trusts it.
  5. The agent roles: scout, reviewer, miner, strategist, replicator, with the 19 axes as the seed queue.
  6. NIFTY: its own data contract, dated tables and SPAN margin.

8. Open checks and contradictions

  • NIFTY settlement, unresolved. The backtester lane says NIFTY settles to the index close, built from the constituents' last 30-minute weighted prices. The alpha and validation lanes say that, since 3 August 2026, F&O stocks' closing prices come from a closing auction. Both may be true at different dates. My check of the NSE page timed out. Read the NSE circular before modelling NIFTY settlement by date.
  • LiveOption's fills, corrected. The harness lane wrote "mid fills plus 1 bp slippage". The paper charges a nominal $0.0001 per contract and tests charging the half-spread separately. Charging the half-spread moved the pooled 0DTE mean from −0.22 to −0.45 percentage points.
  • Numbers from abstracts. Most paper numbers come from abstracts or long extracts. I checked Gençay, LiveOption, Kinlay, ORATS and the Databento expiry trap at the source. Check the others at the link before citing them.
  • Holdout. The 122 fresh days from 8 October have been read once. The next frozen holdout must be days nobody has downloaded yet.
  • Not found: an academic test of vanna or charm flows; a dealer-gamma study on NIFTY; the price of the Dhan Data Plan and Theta Data; method docs for Stockmock and Opstra.
