# 2026-10-01

## From orchestrating the Upstox data feed build (feature #173, PR #184)

- **Headless implementers end when they stop replying.** A `claude -p` worker that starts its test run in the background and waits is cut off mid-ticket (both wave 1 workers did this, one with uncommitted files). The prompt must say: run every command in the foreground, never end the turn to wait, do not run the full suite (the orchestrator does after the merge).
- **`/mattpocock-skills:implement` works as the first words of a headless worker's prompt** on the Pro accounts; the skill loads from the linked plugins folder.
- **`cx.mjs` does not recognise "You've hit your session limit"** (its pattern wants "hit your limit"), so it exits 1, not 10, and does not mark the account spent. On 1 Oct all three accounts hit it this way; they had to be marked spent by hand in `~/.claude/accounts/state.json`. Worth fixing in the script.
- **A Pro account lasts about an hour of Opus implementer work** (a: 14:00 to 15:10, b: 14:05 to 15:05, c: 15:25 to 16:05), then five hours until reset. A worker cut off by the limit usually leaves one complete commit: check it (typecheck, lint, the ticket's tests), merge, and let the review find what is unfinished, rather than restart the ticket.
- **Reviews find real faults when told what to attack.** The useful ones were framed as probes: "the worker was cut off, look for anything unfinished", "try to make the secret land in the capture". Standards and spec reviews run well as read-only `cx` workers on a spare account.
- **A worker can leave a hung pytest behind**, which holds files in the worktree's `.venv` so `git worktree remove` fails with "Invalid argument" on Windows. Look for processes under the worktree path before removing it.
- **Starting the next ticket before its blocker is closed worked** when the blocker's code was already merged and only review fixes were pending: bring the fix commits into the new ticket's branch. It did not work for two tickets editing the same module (#179 after #178, #181 after #179 had to wait for the fixes).
- **Drafting the Guide and glossary early, in parallel with the build, paid off**, but only because the drafter had to list every statement resting on unbuilt tickets (44 of them) and a second pass checked each against the finished code. About a dozen were wrong.
- **`tests/chain/test_volatility.py::test_an_expiry_of_three_hundred_strikes_takes_well_under_a_few_milliseconds` is load-sensitive**: with the desktop app, Herdr and Docker busy (about 40% CPU) it failed three runs out of six alone. It is a wall-clock assertion; worth loosening or marking.
- **Upstox facts settled by the build's reviews**: a contract that has not traded can carry a last trade time of zero as well as 1 January 1980; the message cannot tell "no open interest figure" from zero; the one-use address has two long codes after its question mark (`requestId`, `code`); the subscription request id that worked live is twenty hexadecimal characters; what Upstox answers on the authorize call for a cancelled or expired token is still unknown (the adapter treats only 401 as refused; #183 to confirm).
