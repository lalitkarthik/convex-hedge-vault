Key Discussion Points:

Data Retention and Feed Capacity
The review began with data-engineering concerns around market-data timing, feed separation, and retention. The participants identified Redis memory limits for high-volume option data and favored approximately five minutes of retention over thirty minutes.

Market Timing and Instrument Configuration
The discussion moved to vendor feeds, polling frequency, and the need for accurate market timing by instrument. It also covered instrument-specific multipliers, lot sizes, and broker or adapter subscription lists.

Feed Control and Market-Open Handling
The proposed feed controller would use market timing and market-open information to determine when data should be processed. The discussion emphasized that this timing information must be represented in the design rather than assumed globally.

Execution Signals and Slippage Analysis
The review then addressed the execution layer, including strategy signals, position decisions, signal-time prices, Greeks, implied volatility, and execution prices. Signal-card records were discussed as a basis for measuring slippage and supporting dashboards, while remaining separate from order generation.

Strategy State Persistence and Crash Recovery
The final portion examined how strategy-worker crashes could cause counters and transient state variables to reset. The proposed persistence model included message-based communication, PostgreSQL tables, JSONB state, and checkpoints, but the complete recovery and fallback design was still under consideration.

Broker Data and Reconciliation
The discussion clarified broker capabilities, including limitations around discretionary trading, and established that broker data should be treated as authoritative for holdings and order status. The team identified the need for reconciliation logic, safeguards against endless loops, and additional validation to limit manual intervention.

Order Books and Strategy Mapping
The team reviewed the relationships among Books One through Four, with particular emphasis on connecting Book Three to Book Four. They discussed how working orders, fills, open orders, positions, strategy tags, and combinations of contracts should be represented, while noting that some attribution rules still required definition.

Documentation, Testing, and Implementation
The working book was expected to remain active for tracking purposes, and Book Three documentation was targeted for completion by Friday. The team planned to begin implementation afterward and favored test-driven development, iterative refinement, and testing after each implementation cycle.

Execution Infrastructure
The discussion shifted to selecting a broker adapter for execution and adding an authentication layer. Bilal also referenced reusing an existing connection-control planning approach, and an 8:00–9:00 time window was mentioned without further context.
