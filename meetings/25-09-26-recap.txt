Key Discussion Points:

Trading System Architecture and Account Structure
The participants discussed a common OMS, broker adapters, downstream execution components, and support for multiple broker and client accounts. They considered how client accounts, fund-manager books, execution venues, and broker-specific configurations should fit into the architecture.

Client Subscriptions, Risk Allocation, and Strategy Books
The discussion focused on differing strategy risk levels, client eligibility, subscription-based order placement, and client-specific allocations. The participants also examined strategy units, lot sizing for straddles and ratios, and the need to manage book attribution when multiple strategies produce related orders.

Architecture Planning and Strategy Development
The participants reviewed the architecture-planning timeline and the intention to build execution-engine functionality on the existing design. Strategy development, backtesting, templates, and timing for further work were also discussed.

Backtest-to-Live Execution Discrepancy
The group identified a mismatch between positive backtests and negative live execution results. They attributed the difference to one-minute backtest decisions versus tick-level live decisions, along with sudden price movements and execution slippage.

Latency, Configurable Cadence, and Market Spikes
The participants considered lower-latency and real-time decision-making, configurable decision intervals, and the impact of rapid SPX and option-price movements. They discussed how market-maker order withdrawal, slippage, stop losses, and delayed hedging can degrade live performance. The discussion was still continuing when the transcript ended, including further examination of put behavior.

Volatility Skew and Ratio Structures
Rahul and Meghav discussed inverted and asset-specific skew, with USD-INR described as exhibiting call skew and risky assets generally showing put skew. They explained how ratios and butterflies place positions across the skew curve and can reveal sentiment or benefit when the skew reverses.

Long-Gamma Overlay
Rahul described a session with approximately 10% implied volatility and a $16 straddle, suggesting that such conditions could support a long-gamma strategy. The participants considered pairing limited-downside long gamma with the existing short-gamma book so that either a market move or time decay could contribute to overall performance.

Timing, Decay, and Profit Profile
Bilal and Rahul examined the timing of potential gamma and delta gains from relatively small market moves. They discussed how combining the strategy with existing short gamma could reduce broad losses and produce a more consistent overall profit profile.

Execution and Stop-Loss Risks
The participants reviewed issues affecting short-gamma and ratio trading, including low-movement periods, sudden candles, stop-loss decisions, market-order execution, and options that temporarily expand dramatically in value before returning to their prior range. Bilal cited a stop-loss example involving a fill at 14.9 after a stop level of 9.82.

Nifty Strategy Development
The discussion shifted to Nifty and the effort to improve short-gamma ratio strategies. Rahul referenced directional positioning and protection, while the group worked through strike intervals, premiums, and distances from the market.

System Testing and Capital Allocation
Rahul discussed implementing the approach in the system and conducting approximately one week of backtesting. The participants also considered a second account with roughly $250,000 and reviewed further numerical examples involving strike spacing, premiums, and dollar values.

Volatility Analysis and Strategy Alignment
The participants discussed measuring market moves using sigma bands and evaluating next-day performance after gap-ups. They planned to use the results to align ratios, strangles, and related strategies with a defined confidence level.

Alternative Strategies and Execution Timing

Instrument-Specific Behavior and Delayed Entry

Ratio Strategies, Data, and Model Updates

Multi-Broker Execution and Position Routing
