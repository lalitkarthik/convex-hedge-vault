Key Discussion Points:

Notebook Structure and Forward-Price Modeling
The team reviewed the Jupiter notebook structure and clarified the relationship between iron condor, iron fly, spot price, and forward price calculations. The discussion focused on date-to-expiry differences and converting spot prices to forward prices for valuation and payoff analysis.

Implied Forward from Option-Chain Data
Bilal explained that the forward price can be inferred from option-chain call and put prices using put-call parity. The proposed regression uses call-minus-put values across strikes to derive the discount factor and implied forward, which is then used for ATM and payoff calculations.

Spot Versus Forward Payoff Display
The team examined whether payoff graphs should use spot or forward prices on the horizontal axis. Bilal recommended implementing a toggle that allows users to switch between the two representations, noting that the change primarily shifts the axis.

Straddles, Credit Spreads, and Missing Data
The discussion moved from straddle visualization to the planned addition of credit spreads, which will use the implied forward. Bilal also described handling missing values by using valid strikes only, requiring at least three points for regression where possible, and using direct calculations as a fallback.

Days-to-Expiry Calculation
The team reviewed the calculation of time to expiry using 375 trading minutes per day and 252 trading days per year. The method includes the remaining fraction of the current trading day, illustrated with an example based on elapsed minutes during a Thursday session.

Credit-Spread Payoffs and Interpolation
The team defined credit and debit treatment for credit spreads and reviewed how the net premium is calculated. They then discussed interpolating payoff points, with spline-based methods identified as the likely way to generate smooth curves.

Expiry-Related Modeling Discussion
The participants connected days to expiry with the Black-Scholes model and payoff-curve generation. They discussed holding days to expiry constant while generating points and applying spline interpolation, but the expiry discussion remains in progress at the end of the available meeting segment.

Payoff Graphs and Spread Mechanics
Bilal explained how buying and selling quantities affect payoff graphs and described payoff construction through superimposed naked positions. He introduced debit spreads and contrasted their limited payoff profile and negative theta with the positive theta of credit spreads.

Ratio Spreads and Strike Structures
The discussion covered ratio spreads with different leg ratios, including one-to-three, one-to-four, and one-to-five structures. Bilal explained how leg spacing, strike selection, and changes in the initial strike can create different debit or credit outcomes.

Market Movement, Time Decay, and Structure Selection
Bilal examined how selected long and short leg combinations respond when the market moves, including scenarios that can become negatively exposed. The team discussed choosing structures with greater headroom, managing overshooting risk, and improving risk-adjusted returns.

Straddles, Strangles, and Advanced Combinations
Bilal identified straddles, strangles, and related combinations as more sophisticated strategies that may provide opportunities when fewer market participants use the same approach. He also described how changing strikes and adding naked positions alters the resulting payoff.

Mathematical Modeling and Live Greeks
The discussion shifted to mathematical structures, payoff combinations, and the role of delta, gamma, and vega. The platform is intended to support Nifty, SPX, and potentially Delta Exchange crypto options, with live Greek calculations for each market tick.
